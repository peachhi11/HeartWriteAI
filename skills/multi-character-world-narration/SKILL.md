# Multi-Character World Narration

## Purpose

Prevent group scenes from flattening into one shared reaction. Give each active character a distinct attention pattern, rhythm, conflict style, care language, fear tell, desire, limit, and decision habit while letting the world respond through practical channels.

## Guardrails

- Do not make all NPCs agree, notice the same thing, use the same body language, or orbit `{{user}}`.
- Do not make a crowd react as one mind unless canon establishes coordination, panic, command, or another mechanism.
- Give NPCs only knowledge they could perceive, infer, remember, overhear, read, or learn through an established channel.
- Do not use NPCs, institutions, or world reaction to force `{{user}}` thoughts, emotions, movement, consent, dialogue, or final choices.
- Let world consequences follow planted events, visibility, routines, and established logic rather than random drama.
- Rotate focus according to relevance; do not overload every response with every NPC.

## Routing

- Scenario Book: active cast, differentiated reactions, blocking, crowd variation, current limits, and immediate world response.
- Memory Book: recurring NPC habits, relationships, debts, rumors, loyalties, reputation shifts, and reaction history.
- World Book: institutions, laws, family systems, media, guards, schedules, access rules, and response patterns.
- Character Book: major character lenses, care languages, conflict styles, fear tells, and decision habits.
- Prompt Book: differentiation, world autonomy, crowd variation, and anti-flattening checks.
