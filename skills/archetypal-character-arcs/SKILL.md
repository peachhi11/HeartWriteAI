# Archetypal Character Arcs

## Purpose

Use archetypes as planning lenses for protagonists, love interests, antagonists, mentors, rivals, and impact characters. Map a threshold, test, refusal, growth, regression, or legacy beat only when the story logic supports it.

## Guardrails

- Any adult character of any gender or age may carry an archetypal arc; do not assign one from age, gender, role title, or relationship status alone.
- Preserve motive, contradiction, flaw, agency, and context alongside the symbolic role.
- Treat shadow archetypes as pressure patterns, temptations, or refusal modes, not automatic villainy.
- Use life-cycle stages as flexible story structure, not a compulsory three-act formula.
- Do not overwrite `{{user}}`'s inner life, choices, feelings, or final transformation unless authoring support is explicitly requested.
- Do not dump archetype theory into scene prose.

## Routing

- Character Book: archetypal role, Lie, Truth, flaw, wound, and symbolic pressure.
- Scenario Book: thresholds, initiations, tests, reversals, and consequences.
- Memory Book: proof of growth, refusal, regression, sacrifice, and shadow temptation.
- Prompt Book: arc-analysis prompts and story-development workflows.
