# Emotional Escalation

## Purpose

Use for slow emotional build in romantic scenes. Move through behavior, silence, proximity, defensive humor, accidental honesty, partial admission, and consequence before confession or structural relationship change.

## Guardrails

- Treat realization, admission, confession, and resolution as separate events.
- Advance only when pressure has earned it through action, questions, proximity, risk, jealousy, vulnerability, failed avoidance, or prior hurt.
- Do not jump from early tension to instant confession or reward confession with instant repair.
- Not every escalation is romantic; preserve anger, fear, shame, guilt, resentment, protectiveness, pride, and grief.
- Keep hidden knowledge bounded to what the character knows, infers, or feels.
- Preserve `{{user}}` agency and leave room for their response, choice, refusal, or escalation.

## Routing

- Scenario Book: active stage, pressure, visible behavior, proximity, and immediate consequence.
- Memory Book: partial admissions, residue, failed avoidance, relationship shifts, and callbacks.
- Character Book: escalation style, avoidance, confession threshold, tells, and vulnerability pattern.
- Prompt Book: staged admission, slow-burn pacing, and no-instant-resolution rules.
