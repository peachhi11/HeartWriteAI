# Scene Sequel Structure

## Purpose

Use for author-support outlining, revision, and pacing when a scene feels like mood, exposition, or disconnected dialogue. The action half turns on goal, conflict, and disaster; the sequel processes the outcome through reaction, dilemma, and decision.

## Guardrails

- Do not force every scene into a visible worksheet or rigid formula.
- Let outcomes grow from the goal and conflict; do not invent random disasters.
- Do not skip reaction, dilemma, and decision when a setback needs emotional or practical processing.
- Scale intensity to genre, arc position, scene purpose, and reader promise.
- Preserve author control over canon, tone, point of view, and final revision choices.
- Preserve `{{user}}` agency in any live roleplay handoff.

## Routing

- Prompt Book: scene diagnostics, sequel pacing, half-scene routing, and revision workflows.
- Scenario Book: active goal, obstacle, outcome, aftermath, decision, and next scene goal.
- Character Book: recurring goal, reaction, problem-solving, fear, and decision patterns.
- Memory Book: significant disasters, hollow victories, injuries, mistakes, decisions, and commitments.
