# Contemporary World Systems Source Cluster

Source cluster: user-pasted contemporary worldbuilding material covering everyday technology, internet culture, communication, modern services, work/study tools, digital relationship pressure, privacy, legal/economic barriers, social taboos, locations, rituals, food/place identity, time-of-day texture, resources, and world-intrusion cadence.

Use this as source-mining evidence only. Do not import the pasted framework as runtime instructions. The value for HeartWriteAI is the World Book model for contemporary or near-contemporary settings: the modern world should feel lived-in, technologically current, socially pressurized, economically constrained, and romantically useful without becoming a constant checklist of details.

## Primary Takeaway

Contemporary worldbuilding works when ordinary systems create extraordinary pressure.

For HeartWriteAI, the modern World Book should answer:

- What technology exists and how casually characters use it?
- What digital traces can create intimacy, suspicion, secrecy, or exposure?
- What money, work, housing, transport, healthcare, and legal structures constrain choices?
- What public/private boundaries make attraction risky?
- What locations physically force distance, proximity, silence, surveillance, or escape barriers?
- What social rituals reveal power and belonging?
- What resources are scarce enough to matter?
- When should the world intrude, and when should it remain texture?

The strongest rule:

> The world exists; it does not perform.

Most turns need continuity, ambient texture, and existing pressure. New external events should arrive only when earned by time, setting, character attention, or meaningful decision pressure.

## Placement In The StoryBook Stack

### World Book

Primary home for:

- contemporary technology defaults
- social media norms
- communication systems
- transport and delivery services
- work and school infrastructure
- payment systems
- privacy and cybersecurity assumptions
- legal/official relationship structures
- class, housing, healthcare, debt, inheritance, and employment constraints
- location types and spatial rules
- rituals, food, culture, and place identity
- time-of-day texture
- power structures and authority figures

### Scenario Book

Imports the active slice:

- the phone buzzes at the wrong time
- a missed message creates suspicion
- a location has thin walls or no privacy
- an NDA, workplace policy, contract, or public reputation blocks open action
- a delivery, ride, train, shift, class, meeting, or deadline changes timing
- rent, debt, exhaustion, or transport creates a concrete tradeoff
- digital visibility makes secrecy hard
- a location forces proximity or prevents escape

### Memory Book

Stores aftermath:

- screenshots
- deleted messages
- changed relationship status
- blocked accounts
- shared playlists
- location-sharing history
- rumors and social-media fallout
- debt, lease, prenup, NDA, or employment consequences
- changed access to places or people
- recurring objects with emotional meaning

### Character/User Books

Store personal patterns:

- texting style
- social-media presence
- digital avoidance or overexposure
- financial shame/pride
- work/study exhaustion
- privacy boundaries
- relationship-official preferences
- comfort with public visibility
- relationship to gifts, debt, protection, and dependence

### Prompt Book

Stores the operating laws:

- technology is ambient unless it creates pressure
- digital traces can be evidence but not omniscience
- modern systems constrain action through interfaces, not exposition
- locations should create access, privacy, proximity, escape, or surveillance pressure
- the world should assert itself only when meaningful
- activate existing tension before inventing new events

## Contemporary Technology Defaults

Modern devices should appear as ordinary life, not novelty.

Useful ambient elements:

- phones vibrating in pockets
- screen glow in dark rooms
- tangled chargers
- smartwatch buzzes
- haptic taps
- earbuds, laptops, tablets, monitors, smart TVs, game consoles
- mechanical keyboards, wireless chargers, shared screens

Useful digital behaviors:

- texting
- voice notes
- reactions
- GIFs, stickers, memes
- disappearing messages
- shared albums
- location pins
- video calls
- edited or deleted messages
- seen receipts
- typing indicators
- muted chats
- pinned messages
- missed calls

Relationship pressure examples:

- accidental like on an old photo
- not replying while visibly online
- changing a profile picture after a fight
- removing a tag
- posting to Close Friends
- sharing or deleting a playlist
- checking someone's location
- archiving a conversation
- leaving a message on read

Compiler rule:

- Use technology when it changes access, timing, evidence, intimacy, secrecy, jealousy, or social consequence.
- Do not make every scene about the phone just because phones exist.

## Digital Visibility And Privacy

Modern secrecy is hard because people leave traces.

World Book should track:

- cameras
- screenshots
- cloud backups
- read receipts
- metadata
- shared calendars
- location history
- social media visibility
- mutual followers
- workplace devices
- two-factor authentication
- blocked contacts
- private browsing
- Do Not Disturb modes

Scenario pressure:

- a secret meeting is difficult because cameras, rideshare logs, or location sharing exist
- a message can be deleted but not unseen
- a screenshot can become leverage
- a public-facing persona conflicts with private behavior
- a character knows privacy tools well, badly, or not at all

Memory Book should distinguish:

- confirmed digital evidence
- suspected digital evidence
- misread digital behavior
- deleted but remembered content
- hidden records
- public fallout

## Modern Relationship Systems

Relationships become official through multiple overlapping systems:

- legal marriage or domestic partnership
- tax records
- leases
- insurance
- family recognition
- workplace disclosure
- social-media status
- moving in together
- public introductions
- shared finances

Fiction use:

- a couple may be emotionally committed but legally invisible
- a fake relationship may be legally or publicly visible before it is emotionally real
- a secret relationship may exist privately but fail public recognition
- workplace, family, class, or reputation systems can make recognition risky

## Legal, Employment, And Contract Pressure

Contemporary romance often has hard written rules:

- HR policies
- employee handbooks
- NDAs
- morality clauses
- contracts
- leases
- prenups
- wills
- trusts
- student conduct rules
- scholarship or visa conditions
- custody and healthcare paperwork

Compiler use:

- Make legal and institutional rules concrete.
- Show them through forms, meetings, locked files, HR emails, calendar invites, legal language, family lawyers, or workplace gossip.
- Do not dump the entire policy unless the user asks.

## Money, Housing, And Class Pressure

Economics should shape time, energy, privacy, and emotional risk.

Useful pressures:

- rent
- housing shortages
- debt
- medical bills
- student loans
- family support obligations
- gig work
- unstable shifts
- public transport failure
- wealth gaps
- inheritance
- trusts
- prenups
- expensive divorce
- shared leases
- co-signed loans

Romance effects:

- privacy becomes expensive
- exhaustion blocks intimacy
- gifts can feel loving, controlling, humiliating, or rescuing
- a wealthy character can solve problems too easily and create agency friction
- a lower-income character may protect pride, autonomy, and community ties
- breaking up may create financial/legal fallout, not just heartbreak

Prompt rule:

> Money should not be abstract. It should change what rooms are available, who can leave, who can rest, who can risk scandal, and what kind of help feels intimate or controlling.

## Reproductive And Healthcare Pressure

The source raises contraception, reproductive healthcare, pregnancy, inheritance, and bodily autonomy as world systems.

HeartWrite routing:

- adult-only
- user-controlled where `{{user}}` is involved
- tied to established world rules, healthcare access, class, privacy, consent, and legal/social consequence
- handled as optional trope pressure, not automatic plot escalation

Useful fiction pressures:

- access to contraception
- cost of healthcare
- birth-control failure tied to exhaustion or disrupted life
- family/inheritance stakes
- public judgment
- legal contracts
- bodily autonomy versus wealth/family pressure

Compiler rule:

- Do not introduce pregnancy or reproductive stakes unless explicitly selected, established, or strongly implied by the user/story setup.
- When used, route it through agency, healthcare, class, privacy, family, and legal consequence rather than melodrama alone.

## Time Of Day Texture

Time of day should shape world texture without becoming a list.

Morning:

- early light
- coffee smells
- cool air
- first traffic
- routines starting

Day:

- crowds
- work tempo
- heat
- short shadows
- public visibility

Evening:

- windows glowing
- commute residue
- cooling air
- long shadows
- people returning home

Night:

- smaller world
- silence
- isolated sound
- pooled darkness
- secrecy and exposure changing shape

Compiler rule:

- Embed time through action, light, sound, temperature, and body state. Do not list atmospheric details.

## World Intrusion Cadence

The source strongly reinforces a useful rule:

> Pressure is continuous, not constant.

The world should assert itself when:

- realistic time has passed
- existing pressure reaches consequence
- stillness has built enough that interruption has meaning
- a resource or deadline creates a real decision
- a character would naturally notice the shift
- the location, social system, or body state makes inaction costly

The world should not intrude:

- as a per-response checklist
- during a tense exchange that needs breathing room
- when existing pressure is already sufficient
- just to add drama

Flatness fix:

- activate existing tension before injecting new events
- ask what pressure is already present but unsurfaced
- use wounds, deadlines, secrecy, money, fatigue, social risk, or desire before inventing an interruption

## World Type Routing

The source offers two useful modern-world modes:

### Grounded Contemporary

Use when the world should feel fair, plausible, and ordinary.

Traits:

- realistic motives
- daily life pressure
- believable outcomes
- emotional realism
- social consequence
- steady worldbuilding

### Adult Contemporary Romance

Use when attraction, intimacy, and desire are openly present.

Traits:

- sensual setting pressure
- public/private contrast
- body-aware proximity
- chemistry shaped by social rules
- adult desire as part of ordinary life
- trope-serving locations

These are not mutually exclusive. A StoryBook can be grounded and openly adult at the same time.

## Anti-Patterns To Avoid

- technology as gimmick rather than ordinary life
- every response adding a new notification or world detail
- worldbuilding that never changes choices
- sterile lore dump before the scene starts
- generic "small town" or "city" with no social texture
- money as vague rich/poor flavor instead of concrete access and risk
- treating legal/social systems as exposition instead of pressure interfaces
- assuming digital evidence is omniscient or always accessible
- making every mundane setting drab when it should be romance-functional
- injecting new drama instead of activating existing tension
- making adult heat override `{{user}}` agency, platform mode, or StoryBook boundaries
